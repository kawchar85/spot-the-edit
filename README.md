# Spot the Edit + Verify

**Learn to investigate viral news cards instead of trusting what looks real.**

Spot the Edit + Verify is a planned, Bangla-first (with English support), mobile-friendly media-literacy game. Players encounter simulated social-media news photocards, record their first impression, investigate a fictional publisher's archive and independent evidence, then reach a supported conclusion. An investigation replay shows how evidence changed their thinking.

> **Status:** Planning. The gameplay, content, and pilot described here are specifications, not completed functionality or measured results.

## Why this exists

Convincing news photocards can misrepresent an authentic article, alter a photograph or quotation, or be fabricated from scratch. Visual inconsistencies may be helpful clues, but a professionally rendered card is not proof of authenticity. The aim is to practice **verification behavior**, not merely guessing "real or fake."

## Core loop

1. **See** a photocard in a mock social feed.
2. **Record a first impression:** Looks fine / Suspicious / Can't tell yet.
3. **Investigate (optional, not forced):** inspect the card, visit its fictional outlet's complete *in-game* archive, and consult a separate fictional source.
4. **Conclude:** Supported / Not supported / Insufficient evidence; identify the evidence behind the decision.
5. **Replay:** see the initial judgment, actions taken, final conclusion, and decisive evidence.

Challenges cover edited photos, altered quotations, cleanly fabricated cards, and genuine cards. All scenarios use fictional outlets, fictional people/events, and conspicuous training labeling.

**Important distinction:** verifying that a publisher posted something is not the same as proving the underlying claim is true. A missing article in a real-world archive alone is not proof of fabrication.

## Implementation direction

A static web app with content-driven JSON, reusable HTML/SVG card templates, locally hosted/suitable Bangla fonts, and no required accounts or server for ordinary play. An optional local results export supports small studies without collecting identifiable information.
